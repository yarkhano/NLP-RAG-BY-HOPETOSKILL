# TRAVEL GUIDE: SWAT VALLEY, PAKISTAN

## Introduction and Geography of Swat Valley
The lush green and historic Swat Valley, in the Malakand Division, lies between 34° 40' to 35° N latitude and 72° to 74° 6’ E longitude and is part of the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province of Pakistan. The valley is an integral part of the strategic and significant region where three parts of the Asian Continent—South Asia, Central Asia, and China meet. 

The names found in ancient sources for Swat are Udyana and Suvastu, because of the scenic beauty of the valley and the name of the river, respectively. The historical and cultural remains of the area provide evidence about human activities covering a large span of time. Alexander the Great came here in 327 BC en route to India. In ancient times, it remained a center of Buddhism, with its marks still found from the river bed to the tops of the mountains. 

During the peaceful reigns of Abdul Wadud (1917–1949) and Jahan Zeb (1949–1969), Swati territories forming part of the Swat State witnessed unprecedented development in the fields of education, health, and communication.

The magnificent Swat Valley, popularly known as the Switzerland of the East, is situated north of Peshawar. In ancient times, it remained the center of the then-flourishing Buddhist civilization, with its remnants still found at various places. Its old name was Udyana (Land of Gardens). The archeological sites and monasteries dating back to the Buddhist era add to its historical charm. 

Surrounded by majestic mountains, Swat Valley is full of roaring rivers, lush green forests, and snow-covered peaks adding to its natural attraction. "Upper" and "Lower" Swat are terms in vogue based on the arbitrary demarcation of higher and lower regions in the valley by the Swat River. Green fruit orchards, beautiful lakes, gushing streams, and a fascinating landscape make it a place worth seeing.


## Travel Information and How to Get There
Swat is well connected with important cities like Islamabad, Rawalpindi, and Peshawar via road. 

### Air Route
The twice-weekly flight (a 30-minute journey) to Swat from Islamabad and Peshawar was suspended at the time of compiling this publication. Travelers should check current status with their travel agents.

### Road Route via Malakand
If leaving from Peshawar or Islamabad, the route to Swat is via Mardan. From Mardan, the main road to Swat leads north over the historic Malakand Pass. Swat's distance from Peshawar is 151 km. The distance from Rawalpindi via Nowshera-Mardan and Malakand Pass is 270 km. 

Dargai and Batkhela are the main towns en route until one reaches Chakdara, which is another town steeped in history. Here, the road forks into two: one leading towards Chitral and another towards Swat. The Chakdara Fort guards the area. An important post on a hill is the famed Churchill's Picket (or Piquet), a small fort built in 1896. Winston Churchill once served here as a Daily Telegraph dispatcher, a subject dealt with in much detail in his autobiographical book, "The Story of the Malakand Field Force".

### Security Notice
Visitors and tourists are advised to carry their identification cards/documents with them to avoid inconvenience at routine security checkpoints en route.


## Major Cities and Key Destinations

### Town of Mingora
Mingora (pronounced Mingawara) is the center of economic activities and the only urban area of the valley. It is adjacent to Saidu Sharif. It offers traditional, Pakistani, and Western food outlets, restaurants, and hotels. 

Locally produced goods are beautifully displayed at its main bazaar, shops, and showrooms. These include semi-precious stones, embroidered dresses, hand-woven ladies' shawls, bedsheets, and woodwork. Traditional ornaments and antiques are available at shops. Visitors on trips to Swat Valley usually stroll in its bazaars for shopping at the end of their journey. You can find emerald mines in the hills northward of the city, famous worldwide for their expensive and valuable emeralds.

### Saidu Sharif
Being the capital city of the picturesque Swat Valley, Saidu Sharif is the principal seat of administration for looking after and managing regional government affairs. Several colleges, schools, and government offices are situated here. 

Worth-seeing places in Saidu Sharif include:
- Swat Museum
- The Tomb of the Akhund of Swat (Hazrat Saidu Sharif)
- Beautifully built buildings of the Swat State era, especially Jehan Zeb College
- The wooden building of the Mehmankhana
- The archeological sites of Butkara 1, 2, and 3

### Swat Museum
This museum has a huge and precious collection of Gandhara Art. Artifacts, stupas, coins of various ages, and other assets of the Gandhara civilization signify the rich cultural and historical past of the valley. 

The museum is situated on the main Mingora-Saidu Sharif road. These artifacts, antiques, and similar articles are categorized and beautifully showcased. The cultural life of the near past of the valley is displayed in the form of local dresses, embroidery, jewelry, musical instruments, weapons of old times, and carved wooden articles. Note: The museum is closed on Fridays.

### Malam Jabba Ski Resort
A trip to Swat is incomplete without seeing Malam Jabba, located about 44 km from Mingora. It is a modern hill resort with surrounding mountains enhancing its natural attraction and beautiful landscape. It features a majestic landscape adorned with snowy mountain peaks, lush green valleys, and sprawling forests. 

Malam Jabba is the star destination in Swat for adventure seekers. This modern hill resort, swarming with enthusiastic tourists, once housed a winter ski resort which unfortunately failed to stand the test of time. 

Therefore, in an attempt to restore the grandeur of the area and attract domestic and foreign skiing lovers, the TCKP (Tourism Corporation Khyber Pakhtunkhwa) in September 2015 awarded the restoration of a state-of-the-art ski resort equipped with a new chairlift and a 3-4 star hotel. A year later, the chairlift and skiing resort were inaugurated, while the construction of the hotel entered its final stages. Malam Jabba offers a beautiful landscape of snow-filled mountain peaks, green valleys, and sprawling forests, refreshing minds in a cool and serene atmosphere generously gifted by nature.

### Marghuzar
About 13 km from Saidu Sharif is situated the serene small town of Marghuzar at the foot of Mount Elum. Its White Marble Palace (White Palace), built by the ruler of Swat in 1940, adds to the grandeur of this peaceful small valley. The palace has been converted into a top-class hotel with excellent lodging and boarding facilities. 

While voyaging towards Marghuzar, you can see a road turning towards Salampur, a small village famous for its wool cottage industry.

### Miandam
Encircled by mountains, the picturesque tourist resort of Miandam is at a distance of about 57 km from Saidu Sharif. Surrounding mountains during the summer do not allow the sun to cast its shadows directly on this attractive resort, making it the coolest place in the area. A PTDC motel, a few hotels, and official guest houses keep this beautiful place alive in the summer season. It provides a refreshing view of large forests, fruit orchards, and flowing streams.


## Valleys of Northern Swat

### Madyan
The valley of Madyan is situated about 56 kilometers to the north of Mingora city. It lies on the main road that leads to Bahrain, Kalam, and other beautiful valleys of Swat. Its riverside location and cool climate make it an attractive spot, with rows of shops, hotels, and restaurants queued along the road. A trout hatchery adds to its fascination. Local handicrafts, embroidery, and antiques are displayed at roadside shops.

The slightly wider valley of Madyan is different in comparison to other valleys because almost every type of modern facility and amenity is available here, including internet, cellular networks, cable and satellite TV, hotels, restaurants, and other basic necessities of life. 

Apart from these up-to-date facilities, tall mountains covered with green foliage and dotted with small mud and concrete houses surround the beautiful town of Madyan from three sides. The roaring and foaming Swat River looks like a ferocious monster here, as the huge mountains on both sides appear to fetter and restrain its movement.

### Bishigram Valley
The sprawling valley of Bishigram is the last village on the road. The houses are built like gigantic steps on the uneven landscape of the mountain. The total travel time to reach the valley from Madyan is forty minutes, as the road is not in good condition. Several other small hamlets surround the valley, which can be approached by trekking for an hour and a half. The track to Bishigram Lake starts from this village, which is how it got its name. 

Lush green forests surround the valley, which receives heavy snowfall. Several springs and streams are scattered throughout the valley. Electricity and telephone facilities are available. The area is no longer secluded, and the people visit the nearby town of Madyan almost on a daily basis to sell their goods and buy daily consumable items. Most of the inhabitants live permanently here, while the adjoining hamlets are deserted during winter and occupied again in the summer.

The people are very hospitable and welcome everyone to their beautiful locale. Most rely on farming and grow maize and off-season peas, which fetch lucrative prices in the market. The people speak Pashto and Kohistani languages, with Kohistani being the dominant language in the area. Their dialects are distinct. The people are very traditional, and some old customs related to matrimony are still followed. Some small shops in the area provide daily consumable items. 

Due to the harsh surroundings and lifestyle, the people are physically rough and tough, and their complexions are sunburned as the area is situated at a fairly high altitude above sea level. 

#### Camping Paradise Sites
For the provision of good, clean, and affordable facilities for families, students, and holidaymakers, the Tourism Corporation Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (TCKP) has established unique camping sites at:
- Bishigram (near Madyan, Swat)
- Sharan (in Kaghan)
- Thandiani (near Abbottabad)
- Yakhtangi (Shangla)
- Sheikh Badin (Lakki Marwat)

### Bahrain
This small riverside town, at a distance of 66 km from Saidu Sharif and just 10 km from Madyan, is the most frequently visited resort because of its location on the road leading to other northern resorts. A humming bazaar with shops, eateries, and hotels makes it a popular place. 

Household traditional decoration items are available at shops. Architectural beauty is fully evident in the wooden-made pillars and fixtures of mosques and buildings, arousing the interest of visitors. Nearby valleys, full of lush green thick jungles, offer hiking along narrow mountainous paths.

### Kalam
Being the hometown of Kohistani inhabitants, Kalam is like an earthly paradise in Swat Valley. Located at a distance of 40 km from Bahrain, Kalam is the main town of this northern region, presenting some of the most panoramic natural beauty. The people, known as Kohistani, are its original inhabitants, proud of their own language and still-unchanged traditions. 

A fascinating view of the 6,471m Falaksier Peak with its melting snow during summers from Matiltan (3,000m) is breathtaking. The journey is incomplete without seeing and going to Mahodand, a famous lake located about 23 km beyond Matiltan. 

The picturesque Kalam provides road access to reach the magnificent valleys of:
- Ushu (2,286m above sea level)
- Utrot (2,225m above sea level)
- Gabral (2,286m above sea level)

The journey through these green forests and beautiful landscapes is memorable. The rivers and lakes at Kalam offer good trout fishing, with permits obtainable from the Fisheries Department.

### Mankial Valley
The beautiful valley of Swat is a mix of different and varied valleys and plains. The southern region is comparatively wide and open, consisting of plains that are cultivated throughout the year and yield extremely large quantities of fruit and crops. 

The Northern region is a congregation of tall, high-rising mountains where small valleys provide little ground for cultivation and residential space for the small population scattered in different groups and families. The valley of Mankial is one such place, located in the north of Swat on the main road leading to Kalam.

The total distance of the valley from Saidu Sharif is about eighty kilometers. Although the condition of the road is not good, the scenic beauty along the Swat River keeps visitors engaged. The fascinating valley of Mankial is famous throughout Swat for its several sharp and jagged summits or peaks, which can be seen hundreds of kilometers away from down the plains. The tallest peak in the Mankial range stands at an altitude of 18,750 feet above sea level.
